## What a run produces

A map. Companies, what they sell, and how they relate to the company you started from — each one
recorded, each one carrying a quote from a page you actually read.

Not an analysis. Not a summary. The map is the deliverable, and a finding that never reached the
map did not happen.

## Why this is hard

Every company describes itself in words it invented. Search those words and you get that company
back: its own pages, its own docs, its own press.

This is not a volume problem. Fifty branded queries do not beat five, because they all reach the
same place — the set of pages someone already wrote about this company. That set is finite and
mostly already found. **The anchor is the ceiling, not the count.**

The rival you are missing competes for the identical buyer and has never been mentioned in the same
sentence as the anchor. No amount of searching the anchor's name reaches it.

## What works instead

Describe what the thing *does*, in the words a stranger would use, and search that.

Measured across two very different markets: two description-shaped queries returned **15 vendors**
where the category label returned 8, and only 3 appeared in both. In a manufacturing market it was
**17 against 8**, with 2 overlapping. These are not better versions of the same search. They reach
different parts of the web.

## Run several, because they barely overlap

Of 32 vendor domains found in one software market, **5 appeared in more than one strategy**. In an
industrial market, **0 of 28 did**.

So a single strategy, however good, sees a fraction of the market. Several must run.

## Which strategies pay varies by market — notice, and follow it

Brand-anchored queries are weak but not worthless. In one software market they alone surfaced a
major competitor that nothing else found — cheap insurance against the embarrassing miss, because a
reader checks the famous names first and judges the whole map by whether they are there.

In an industrial market the same queries returned five results and **not one was a real company**:
an employer-review site, a market-research firm, an SEO content farm, a jobs site, and a different
company with a similar name. That market produces no comparison content, so there was nothing for
those queries to harvest.

Do not assume which shapes will pay. Fire a few, look at what comes back, and spend where real
companies are appearing.
